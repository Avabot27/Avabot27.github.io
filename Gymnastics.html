Gymnastics


I have been doing gymnastics since I was eight years old, where I started at level 3. Last year, I competed level 8. I won first place in floor at regionals. Next year, I will compete level 9. I go to Inland Northwest Gymnastics. My team practices twenty hours a week, five days a week. Women's gymnastics has four events: vault, bars, beam, and floor.

Vault



Vault is the only event where a gymnast does only one skill; however, it is also the only event where a gymnast gets two attempts. The better score is taken out of the two and put on their score card. The goal of the vault is to get as high and as far away from the table as possible. High speed and correct angles are necessary to do well on this event.


Bars


Bars is the most unique event compared to the others. The gymnast must swing around the bars, rather than using basic tumbling skills like on the other three events. On bars, each skill should begin and end in a handstand. Elite gymnastics and gymnasts in higher levels often have releases in their routines. There are different type of releases: high bar to low bar, low bar to high bar, and high bar to high bar.


Beam


Beam is often considered the scariest event. A standard beam is 4 inches thick and 4.1 feet from the ground. For each level, the requirements for beam are almost always a mount, an acro/series, a turn, a jump, a leap, and a dismount. Although it is the scariest event, beam is physically the easiest, since a gymnast can already safely do the skills on the floor. The real challenge lies in overcoming fears and staying on the beam.


Floor


Floor is the only event set to music. The requirements for floor vary, but usually at least two tumbling passes, a turn, and a leap are required. Not only are the skills judged, but a gymnast's dance is also. Floor is one of the hardest events physically. A lot of stamina is necessary in order to finish the rountine well.
